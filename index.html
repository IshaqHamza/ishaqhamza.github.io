Bio

I am Ishaq Hamza, an Operations Research PhD student at the Edwardson School of Industrial Engineering, Purdue University, working with Dr. Zaiwei Chen.

Research

I wish to achieve artificial general intelligence through the lens of reinforcement learning, with a focus on developing it as a tool beyond its classical applications, assumptions, and modeling choices. My research so far revolves around data-driven learning algorithms for decision-making in stochastic environments, especially ones that are practical to implement but whose theoretical behavior remains unclear because the analysis is mathematically challenging. I design and analyze such algorithms and prove performance guarantees for them under realistic assumptions, aiming for theory that gives practitioners real insight, rather than bounds that improve on paper but change nothing in practice. Where possible, I also aim for analysis techniques that carry over to similar settings across machine learning and decision-making.

I really like to make sense of random stuff. While my work is mostly theoretical, I like to explore applications where decision problems in nuanced, and perhaps noisy, stochastic environments show up naturally. I also love to discuss ideas in a way that blurs the boundary between formal discussion and casual talk.

Teaching

Advanced Convex Optimization

Jan–Apr 2025

Teaching Assistant, Department of Electrical Engineering, Indian Institute of Science
Instructor: Dr. Kunal N. Chaudhury (course webpage)

A graduate course taken by PhD students, which I assisted during the third year of my undergraduate studies. I conducted tutorials, answered students’ questions, and graded assignments. The course covered rigorous convex analysis in extended-real settings and the operator-theoretic treatment of optimization algorithms in both smooth and non-smooth settings, using set-valued analysis for proximal algorithms.
